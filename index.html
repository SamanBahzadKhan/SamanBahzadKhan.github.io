I am a Computer Science undergraduate at LUMS, working toward a PhD application in December 2026. Driven by a deep enthusiasm for research, I am broadly curious across many areas of computer science — from systems and distributed computing to machine learning and AI alignment.

My current work sits at the intersection of LLM-based systems, preference optimization, and AI-driven automation. I collaborate with Dr. Ali Anwar at the University of Minnesota, and with advisors at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Virginia Tech, and Google:

  • Sem-DPO: Mitigating Semantic Inconsistency in Preference Optimization (Findings of ACL 2026) — a weighted Direct Preference Optimization approach that reduces semantic drift during automated prompt optimization. It improved CLIP similarity by 8–12% and human-preference scores by 5–9% over standard DPO, and was preferred in 82.2% of pairwise human evaluations. Advisors: Dr. Ali Anwar and Dr. Ahmad Faraz Khan (Google).

  • LADs: Leveraging LLMs for AI-Driven DevOps (First Author, MLSys ‘27 submission, PDF) — a six-agent framework for configuration, deployment, monitoring, and automated repair. It reached 68% deployment success across 180 Dask, Redis, and Ray tasks and repaired 94% of runtime failures within three iterations. Advisors: Dr. Ali Anwar and Dr. Ali R. Butt (Virginia Tech).

  • SkillGraph: Reusable Reasoning Skills from LLM Agent Traces (First Author, MLSys ‘27 submission) — a GraphRAG-based skill graph for extracting and reusing diagnostic reasoning from agent trajectories, with a process-level critic that evaluates reasoning quality rather than final outcomes alone. Advisors: Dr. Tianyin Xu (UIUC) and Dr. Ali Anwar.

  • Agent Memory: Storage and Retrieval of LLM Reasoning Traces at Scale (MLSys ‘27 submission) — an analysis of 535 queries across seven models, with reasoning traces ranging from under 1 KB to over 71 KB per query, toward co-location and indexing for highly variable semantic workloads. Advisor: Dr. Ali Anwar.

  • Federated Skill Sharing for LLM Agents (MLSys ‘27 submission) — a federated framework in which clients share reusable skill templates and generalized reasoning patterns through a global indexed library while keeping private context and final adaptation local. Advisors: Dr. Ali R. Butt (Virginia Tech) and Dr. Ahmad Faraz Khan (Google).

Outside of research, I was a summer intern at Systems Limited, where I built an ERP workflow for procurement, project tracking, and reporting. Selected projects include the LLM Affordability Index and the CAPS Counselling Appointment System. I also review papers for IEEE and ACM conferences.

Research

  1. ACL
    Sem-DPO: Mitigating Semantic Inconsistency in Preference Optimization for Prompt Engineering
    Anas Mohamed, Azal Ahmad Khan, Xinran Wang, and 5 more authors
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2026, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Sem-DPO is a weighted Direct Preference Optimization approach that reduces semantic drift during automated prompt optimization and establishes a theoretical bound on drift in preference-tuned prompt generators. Across three benchmarks and three language-model backbones, it improves CLIP similarity by 8–12% and human-preference scores by 5–9% over standard DPO, and is preferred in 82.2% of pairwise human evaluations.

  3. MLSys
    SkillGraph: Reusable Reasoning Skills from LLM Agent Traces
    Saman Bahzad Khan and others
    In Conference on Machine Learning and Systems (MLSys), 2027
    Under Submission. First author.
    Abs Bib HTML

    SkillGraph is a GraphRAG-based skill graph for extracting and reusing diagnostic reasoning from agent trajectories. The project builds a database of reasoning traces for skill extraction and retrieval on new system incidents, and uses a process-level critic that evaluates reasoning quality rather than final outcomes alone.
